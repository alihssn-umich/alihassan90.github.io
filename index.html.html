About

What I do, and where I do it best

I'm an energy systems researcher and engineer working on the problems that decide whether the grid transition actually pencils out: how storage gets integrated, how degraded EV batteries get valued and dispatched, and how storage assets earn their keep in real electricity markets.

My doctoral research at Michigan builds degradation-aware optimization for second-life battery systems — modeling how batteries age, then optimizing their use for energy arbitrage, peak shaving, EV charging support, and backup power for AI data centers. Alongside the modeling, I validate on real hardware through power hardware-in-the-loop (PHIL) testing, and I've contributed to national-scale infrastructure assessment with Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

Before Michigan, I spent four years as a Research Associate and Adjunct Lecturer at COMSATS University Islamabad, where I applied deep learning to power system protection — delivering phasor estimation 5× faster at 98% accuracy versus conventional techniques. I also review for leading journals and conferences in power systems and energy engineering.
